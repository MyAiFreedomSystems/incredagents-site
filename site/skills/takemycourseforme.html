The one rule that saves you hours

Never Headless. Your Real Chrome Does The Walking.

Skool hides every asset behind signed URLs that only your logged-in session can produce. A headless browser gets a 403 on everything. So the skill drives your actual Chrome tab — the one you already logged into — and Circle gets the same bridge or a cookie import.

The Bridge.

JavaScript runs inside your live, logged-in Chrome tab through a small AppleScript bridge. Skool's signed URLs work because your session made them. No headless dead ends, no 146-byte MissingKey errors.

The Manifest.

The full course tree is mapped to a JSON checkpoint before anything downloads. Capture is resumable — lose the connection at lesson 19, restart at lesson 19.

The Real Deliverables.

Lesson text is nice. The downloadable ZIPs and PDFs the course actually sells you are the point — every resource link gets captured, per lesson.

After the capture

Notes First. Then A Handoff Your Team Can Build From.

Eight steps, four of them automated by the bundled scripts. The agent reads every lesson line-by-line — it never summarizes a summary — then cuts only what's missing.

Scannable By Design.

A TLDR box, one bullet per module, and the numbers that matter. Built for an ADHD brain: the whole course on one page, in words a human would use.

Gap Analysis, Not Busywork.

The handoff compares the course against what your team already has and lists only the real gaps to build — with the "already have" pile named so nobody rebuilds it.

Honest Boundaries.

For personal study of content you paid for. The skill says so in its own instructions, and the notes stay yours.
